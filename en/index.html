Who we are

DIDAR — a place to meet, learn, create and connect.

DIDAR is a Montréal-based cultural, educational and community initiative. We bring people together through shared learning, artistic and cultural expression, dialogue and practical mutual support.

We welcome people who want to participate, exchange knowledge and build connections. Some programs respond to specific experiences and community needs, including those of newcomers, international students and underrepresented groups.

Our approach grows from community relationships, listening and solidarity. We do not replace professional or regulated services.

What guides us

Our Mission

DIDAR's mission is to create an independent, welcoming and participatory space where people from diverse backgrounds, experiences and identities can come together, learn from one another, share cultures and knowledge, and contribute to cultural and community life.

DIDAR works to preserve and transmit cultural and linguistic heritage, expand access to education and the arts, encourage creativity, strengthen intergenerational and intercultural connections, and create opportunities for participation and mutual support.

We also seek to challenge discrimination and barriers to participation, including those that may be reproduced within cultural and community spaces. Through education, dialogue and collaboration, we encourage critical reflection, individual autonomy and meaningful participation without requiring people to abandon their cultural identities.

We seek to build bridges between the past and the future, between cultures, and between people and opportunities within society. Our activities are guided by human dignity, equality, shared learning, and social and environmental responsibility.

02

The future we envision

Our Vision

We envision a society where cultural, linguistic, generational and individual differences are not barriers to connection, but opportunities for discovery, learning and collective enrichment.

We imagine a future where cultural heritage is preserved and reimagined; knowledge, education and the arts are accessible to more people; and no one is excluded from participation because of their background, identity or personal circumstances.

DIDAR aspires to become a lasting community space in Montréal and Québec where people feel welcome, strengthen their ability to act, create, engage in dialogue and build meaningful connections across cultures, generations and communities.

We envision communities where belonging does not require conformity or silence, and where individuals can express their identities, question established norms and participate without fear of discrimination or exclusion.

03

Our shared foundation

Our Core Values

Ten principles that shape how DIDAR learns, collaborates, preserves heritage and builds community.

01

Human Dignity, Equality, Equity and Inclusion

We recognize the dignity and equal rights of every person, regardless of origin, language, economic circumstances, age, gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, religious or non-religious beliefs, disability or immigration status.

We are committed to challenging discrimination, whether interpersonal, institutional or systemic, and to reducing barriers to meaningful participation. We recognize that exclusion and unequal power relationships can also exist within our own cultural and community spaces.

02

Cultural Heritage, Memory and Continuity

We value languages, literature, arts, collective memory, oral histories and lived experiences. Preserving heritage does not mean remaining in the past; it means making room for its transmission, reinterpretation and connection with future generations.

We believe cultural heritage is living and evolving. Its preservation should respect human dignity, individual freedom, equality and non-discrimination. Traditions can be celebrated, questioned and reimagined through respectful dialogue.

03

Education, Knowledge and Creativity

We see education, curiosity, research, the arts and knowledge-sharing as pathways to individual and collective growth. Everyone has the potential to learn, teach, share and contribute to new knowledge.

We encourage critical thinking, evidence-informed learning, creativity and the freedom to question established ideas, including our own assumptions and practices.

04

Intercultural Dialogue and Respect for Differences

We value cultural diversity and create opportunities for encounters, dialogue and collaboration across and within cultures, languages and communities. No individual should be expected to represent an entire culture or community.

We recognize that differences in experience, opportunity and power can affect whose voices are heard. We seek to encourage respectful disagreement, mutual understanding and meaningful participation, particularly for people whose perspectives are often overlooked or marginalized.

05

Solidarity, Voluntary Mutual Support and Shared Responsibility

We believe in the power of human relationships and cooperation. Sharing experiences, time, skills and knowledge can create new opportunities for others.

Mutual support is grounded in respect, voluntary participation and collective solidarity. Receiving assistance must never be conditional on repayment, public gratitude, personal loyalty or future contributions. We seek to avoid practices that create dependency or unequal power relationships. We also respect the time, capacities and personal boundaries of those who volunteer and contribute.

06

Empowerment and Personal Autonomy

We recognize that individuals and communities already possess knowledge, strengths and abilities. We seek to reduce barriers that limit their opportunities to learn, make informed decisions, participate and influence matters that affect their lives.

We respect each person's autonomy, freedom of choice and right to define their own path, without imposing expectations about how they should live, participate or express their identity.

07

Freedom of Thought, Pluralism and Institutional Independence

DIDAR is not affiliated with any political party or religious organization. We respect freedom of thought, conscience, belief and expression, including the right to question ideas, traditions and institutions, while recognizing the rights and dignity of others.

Our institutional independence does not mean indifference to discrimination, violence or violations of human dignity. We remain committed to human rights, equality and social justice without requiring participants to share particular political or religious beliefs.

08

Care, Consent, Personal Boundaries and Accountability

We strive to create safer, respectful and inclusive environments, free from harassment, intimidation and abuse of power.

Informed and freely given consent, privacy, personal boundaries and the right to decide how one participates or is represented are fundamental to our approach.

We recognize our responsibility to listen to concerns, reflect on the consequences of our actions and respond fairly and without retaliation when harm or misconduct is reported. No one should be pressured to disclose personal experiences or identities.

09

Environmental Responsibility

We consider environmental protection a shared responsibility. We encourage waste reduction, reuse, repair, recycling, resource-sharing and the adoption of sustainable practices in our programs and everyday activities.

10

Collaboration, Transparency and Collective Accountability

We value cooperation among individuals, artists, educators, researchers, cultural and educational institutions, and community organizations.

Transparency, accountability, responsible use of resources and respect for volunteer contributions are essential to building collective trust.

We promote fair and transparent decision-making, recognition of contributions, responsible management of conflicts of interest and opportunities for feedback. Partnerships and financial contributions must not compromise DIDAR's independence, mission or commitment to the communities it serves.

04

How we put our values into practice

The DIDAR Charter

Preamble

DIDAR is founded on the belief that human connection, education, culture and collective participation can help create a more welcoming, inclusive, equitable and connected society.

This Charter sets out the shared principles and commitments that guide DIDAR's activities, partnerships, decisions and relationships. It serves as a reference for team members, volunteers, partners and participants according to their respective roles and responsibilities.

We recognize that dignity, inclusion and accountability require more than statements of principle. They must be reflected in our everyday actions, organizational practices and willingness to learn and improve.

This text sets out DIDAR’s proposed shared commitments. Formal adoption by the team has not been confirmed.

Choose an article to read its complete text, or expand all eleven.

Article 01

Human Dignity, Equality and Non-Discrimination

Everyone has the right to be treated with dignity and respect, without discrimination. DIDAR opposes racism, sexism, xenophobia, homophobia, transphobia, ableism and all other forms of discrimination and social exclusion.

We recognize that discrimination may be direct, indirect, interpersonal, institutional or systemic, and that it can occur both between and within cultural and community groups.

We are committed to identifying and reducing barriers to meaningful participation, recognizing unequal power relationships and ensuring that our own practices reflect the principles of equality, equity and inclusion.

Article 02

A Respectful and Harassment-Free Environment

We do not tolerate harassment, threats, intimidation, violence, humiliation, coercion or abuses of power.

We strive to create safer, respectful environments in which people can participate without fear of discrimination, intimidation or retaliation.

We commit to listening to concerns, addressing problematic situations fairly and responsibly, respecting the rights of those involved and taking appropriate measures to prevent harm.

We recognize that positions of authority or influence carry particular responsibilities and must not be used to silence, pressure or exploit others.

Article 03

Consent, Autonomy and Privacy

Participation in DIDAR's activities and the sharing of personal experiences are voluntary. No one is required to disclose personal information, experiences or identities as a condition of belonging or participation, except for information genuinely necessary for a particular activity.

Publishing a person's name, photograph, recorded voice, personal story or other identifiable information requires appropriate, informed and freely given consent.

Individuals should be informed about how their information will be used and how they may request the correction or removal of material published by DIDAR, subject to applicable limitations.

We are committed to collecting only necessary personal information, respecting confidentiality and managing personal data responsibly and in accordance with applicable privacy obligations.

Article 04

Cultural Diversity, Freedom of Thought and Respectful Dialogue

We recognize the equal dignity of all people, regardless of their cultural, linguistic or personal identities. We respect the diversity of beliefs and worldviews while maintaining our commitment to human rights, equality and non-discrimination.

We respect freedom of thought, conscience, belief and expression, including the right to question and critically examine ideas, traditions, institutions and established practices.

We encourage respectful dialogue both between and within communities, recognizing that individuals belonging to the same cultural or linguistic community may have different experiences, identities and perspectives.

Disagreement and criticism of ideas must not become harassment, intimidation, discrimination or attacks on people's dignity.

No individual should be expected to represent an entire culture or community.

Article 05

Heritage, Knowledge and Creative Expression

We encourage the preservation, transmission and sharing of cultural heritage, languages, literature, traditional arts, oral histories, collective memory and intergenerational experiences.

We recognize cultural heritage as living and evolving. Traditions can be celebrated, questioned, reinterpreted and adapted through respectful dialogue.

The preservation of cultural heritage must remain consistent with human dignity, individual freedom, equality and non-discrimination. Cultural practices must not be used to justify exclusion, coercion or violations of fundamental rights.

We support creativity, innovation, accessible education and participation in artistic, cultural and scientific activities.

We respect the voices, knowledge, authorship and contributions of the people and communities whose cultural heritage is shared or represented.

Article 06

Voluntary Participation and Mutual Support

DIDAR is built on voluntary participation, cooperation and collective solidarity.

Individuals may contribute according to their interests, abilities, circumstances and availability. Participation and contribution must remain free from coercion or undue pressure.

Receiving community assistance does not create an obligation to repay, express public gratitude, demonstrate loyalty or make future contributions. Participation in DIDAR's activities is voluntary, and any applicable fees or participation requirements must be communicated clearly in advance.

We respect the autonomy, time, personal boundaries and capacities of participants, volunteers and contributors.

Mutual support should strengthen people's independence, dignity and ability to make their own decisions rather than create dependency or unequal relationships.

Article 07

Accessibility and Reducing Barriers

We seek to identify and reduce linguistic, economic, social, cultural, digital, physical and cognitive barriers to participation within our available resources.

Our activities should, as far as possible, take into account participants' different circumstances, abilities, needs and responsibilities.

We recognize French as Québec's common and official language while valuing multilingualism, linguistic diversity and the preservation of heritage languages.

We encourage accessible communication, meaningful participation and opportunities for individuals to express their needs without unnecessary disclosure of sensitive personal information.

Inclusion requires more than welcoming diverse participants; it also involves creating opportunities for their perspectives to be heard and considered.

Article 08

Political and Religious Independence

DIDAR is an independent, non-partisan and non-denominational organization. It is not affiliated with any political party or religious institution.

DIDAR's activities must not be used for partisan or religious propaganda or recruitment.

This institutional independence does not restrict the personal beliefs, identities or freedom of conscience of participants, provided their conduct respects DIDAR's Charter.

Nor does independence mean withdrawing from social issues or remaining silent in the face of injustice.

Defending human dignity, equality, human rights and individual freedoms, and challenging discrimination and violence, are consistent with DIDAR's identity and commitments.

We distinguish between supporting universal human rights and endorsing a particular political party, religious organization or ideology.

Article 09

Environmental Responsibility and Resource Use

In planning and carrying out our activities, we encourage the reduction of unnecessary consumption, reuse, repair, recycling, conservation of resources and environmentally responsible choices.

We promote collective and sustainable practices while taking into account accessibility, affordability and the different needs of participants.

We recognize environmental responsibility as part of our broader commitment to social well-being and the responsible use of shared resources.

Article 10

Transparency, Accountability and Recognition of Our Limits

We are committed to transparency regarding our objectives, activities, roles, resources, decisions and limitations, while respecting confidentiality and privacy.

We welcome constructive criticism, feedback and opportunities to improve our practices. Team members and participants should be able to raise concerns and question decisions without fear of retaliation.

We recognize our responsibility to acknowledge mistakes, address problems fairly and take appropriate corrective measures when necessary.

We strive to communicate honestly about our activities and achievements, distinguishing between completed work, ongoing initiatives and future aspirations.

DIDAR does not replace specialized medical, psychological, legal, immigration or other regulated professional services. When appropriate and within our capacity, we help individuals identify qualified professionals or relevant community resources.

Article 11

Responsible Partnerships and Independence

DIDAR's collaborations must align with its mission, values and commitments to human dignity, inclusion and community interests.

When selecting partners and supporters, we prioritize independent decision-making, mutual respect, transparency, accountability and responsible management of conflicts of interest.

We value and seek to recognize contributions from individuals, volunteers, artists, professionals, businesses and organizations, whether artistic, educational, material, financial or service-based.

Recognition of a contribution does not grant special authority over DIDAR's decisions, nor does a partnership or financial contribution imply endorsement of every position, product, service or activity of a supporter.

We respect the intellectual property, authorship and agreed conditions associated with creative contributions.

DIDAR reserves the right to decline or reconsider collaborations that are incompatible with its mission and ethical commitments, using fair and responsible decision-making processes.

Our guiding promise

Our Shared Commitment

DIDAR is more than a name. It is an invitation to meet, listen to different voices, learn from one another's experiences, preserve and reimagine cultural heritage, and build connections that enrich individual and collective life.

We believe that cultural heritage and individual freedom can enrich one another, that differences can create opportunities for learning, and that belonging should never require the abandonment of one's dignity, identity or autonomy.

We recognize that respect, inclusion and solidarity are not achieved through declarations alone. They require us to examine our assumptions, challenge discrimination, acknowledge unequal power relationships, share responsibility and remain accountable for our actions.

At DIDAR, our differences are opportunities to learn, question, create and grow together—with dignity, freedom, mutual respect and shared responsibility.
